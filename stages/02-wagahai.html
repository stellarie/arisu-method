Stage 2 of 8 · your first real novel

吾輩は猫である

Start with a hard book on purpose. After it, every other book feels lighter.

Why it works

  • You learn to read with gaps

    Real Japanese always has words that you do not know. This book has a lot of them. It trains you to keep going anyway, and that skill matters more than any word list.

  • Your dictionary gets fast

    You look up many words on every page. Each lookup takes less time than the last one. After a few chapters, the lookups feel automatic.

  • Your scale resets

    夏目漱石 published this novel as a serial from 1905 to 1906. Its style is old and dense. After it, a modern novel feels easy. You start stage 3 with real confidence.

What it feels like

It feels slow. One page can take an hour, and some lines stay unclear after three readings. This is normal, and it is part of the method.

The first line helps you a little: 吾輩は猫である。名前はまだ無い。 The cat has no name yet, and you have no vocabulary yet. You are even.

Crawl if you must. One sentence in a day still counts. Slow is fine. The cat has all day too~ (=^･ω･^=)

Signs it is working

  • You look up fewer words on each page.
  • You guess meanings from context more often.
  • You stop panicking at old kanji. You just look them up.
  • You want to know what the cat does next.

Look at you, reading a classic. So proud. Not that I care~ (｡•̀ᴗ-)✧

Cute mistakes to skip

  • Do not swap to an easier book after ten pages. The difficulty is the lesson.
  • Do not translate every sentence into English. Understand it, then move on.
  • Do not count the pages that are left. Count the pages that you finished.

Ten pages and you want to quit? Weak~ Come back and crawl ♡

How to look things up

The cat has many words that you do not know. You look up most of them, so the lookup must be fast. Fast means: point, read, keep going. Never type a word, and never write a word down.

  • Popup first. Read the Aozora text in your browser with Yomitan. Hover a word, hold Shift, read the meaning, release. Start with the Jitendex dictionary. Add JMnedict for names, because the cat meets many people.
  • Outside the browser? If you read in an e-reader or an app, chibipop does the same thing on the screen. Same dictionary files, same hover.
  • Web dictionary second. When the popup gives five meanings and none fits, open Jisho and search the whole sentence. It splits the sentence into words for you.
  • Japanese dictionary later. After about a third of the book, add a Japanese-to-Japanese dictionary such as Kotobank or Weblio. The English gloss starts to feel thin, and a Japanese definition gives you the real shape of the word.

Typing each word into a search box? That is not reading. That is data entry. Hover, silly~

The step: Read 吾輩は猫である from start to end, at any speed. The full text is free on Aozora Bunko.

  • You finished the book. At any speed. The last page counts, not the clock.
  • A page takes minutes now, not an hour.
  • You guess a word from context before you look it up.

Finished the cat? Took you long enough. I was not counting. Much~ (=^･ω･^=)
