Stage 4 of 8 · four kanji, one idea

四字熟語

Each four-character idiom packs four kanji, their readings, and a whole idea into one small unit. Learn at least 200 to 300 of them by heart.

Why it works

  • High density

    One idiom teaches four kanji at once. 一石二鳥 gives you one, stone, two, and bird, plus a meaning that you can use. That is a lot of Japanese for one small unit.

  • Readings come for free

    Most idioms use the Chinese-style readings of their kanji. You learn these readings in context, not from a list. Later, new words with the same kanji feel familiar.

  • A bridge to classical Chinese

    Many idioms come from old Chinese texts, and Japan made others too. The Chinese ones are classical Chinese in miniature. Stage 5 and stage 8 build on exactly this.

What it feels like

The dictionary dive feels like a game. You open one idiom, it links to another, and an hour is gone.

Memorizing the meaning feels hard. The four kanji are easy to see and easy to forget. Say each idiom out loud, and use it in a sentence of your own.

一期一会: one time, one meeting. Treat each idiom like that, and do not waste it~

Signs it is working

  • You see four kanji in a row and you try to read them as one idiom.
  • You guess the meaning of a new idiom from its kanji.
  • Kanji that you never studied start to look familiar.
  • You count past 200, and you still want more.

Past 200 already? Fine. You are a little impressive~ (*ﾉωﾉ)

Cute mistakes to skip

  • Do not only collect idioms. Learn each meaning by heart.
  • Do not skip the reading. An idiom that you cannot say is half learned.
  • Do not stop at 50 because they "feel enough". The target is at least 200 to 300.

Fifty? Cute. Come back at 200~

What a 四字熟語 really is

There is an old classroom joke. A teacher writes （　）肉（　）食 on the board and asks the class to complete the idiom. The answer is 弱肉強食, the strong eat the weak. One student writes 焼肉定食, the grilled meat set meal. Four kanji, correct grammar, and a real word. Still wrong.

That joke tells you what a 四字熟語 is. It is not any four kanji in a row. It is four kanji that carry a story, a picture, or a judgment that everyone agrees on. 弱肉強食 is a law of nature in four strokes. 焼肉定食 is lunch.

Where they come from

Dictionaries sort them into a few families. Some come from Chinese classics, so each one is a tiny classical Chinese sentence: 温故知新 is a line from the Analects, folded flat. Some are Buddhist terms. Some were made in Japan. Some describe modern life. When you learn one, you learn which family it belongs to, and the family tells you how to read it.

How to read one

  • Split it in two. Most 四字熟語 are two pairs: 一石 + 二鳥, 弱肉 + 強食.
  • Ask what the two pairs do to each other. Cause and effect, contrast, or a list of two things.
  • Find the picture. 画竜点睛 is a painter who adds the last dot to the eye of a dragon. The picture stays when the definition fades.

Why they matter on this road

四字熟語 are the smallest unit of classical Chinese that modern Japanese still uses every day. They teach you how classical Chinese packs meaning, before stage 5 asks you to read a whole sutra of it. And in stage 8, many of them exist in Chinese with the same characters. You are learning two languages at once, and only paying for one.

焼肉定食 is still my favorite answer. Do not tell the teacher~

The step: Dictionary-dive yoji.jitenon.jp. Learn at least 200 to 300 four-character idioms by heart, with their meanings.

  • You know at least 200 idioms by heart: four kanji, the reading, and the meaning.
  • You can use ten of them in your own sentences without a pause.
  • You see a new one in the wild, and you guess it right from the kanji.
