Stage 4 of 8 · four kanji, one idea

四字熟語

Each four-character idiom packs four kanji, their readings, and a whole idea into one small unit. Learn 200 to 300 of them by heart.

Why it works

  • High density

    One idiom teaches four kanji at once. 一石二鳥 gives you one, stone, two, and bird, plus a meaning that you can use. That is a lot of Japanese for one small unit.

  • Readings come for free

    Most idioms use the Chinese-style readings of their kanji. You learn these readings in context, not from a list. Later, new words with the same kanji feel familiar.

  • A bridge to classical Chinese

    Many idioms come from old Chinese texts. Their structure is classical Chinese in miniature. Stage 5 and stage 8 build on exactly this.

What it feels like

The dictionary dive feels like a game. You open one idiom, it links to another, and an hour is gone.

Memorizing the meaning feels hard. The four kanji are easy to see and easy to forget. Say each idiom out loud, and use it in a sentence of your own.

一期一会: one time, one meeting. Treat each idiom like that, and do not waste it~

Signs it is working

  • You see four kanji in a row and you try to read them as one idiom.
  • You guess the meaning of a new idiom from its kanji.
  • Kanji that you never studied start to look familiar.
  • You count past 200, and you still want more.

Do not be that learner

  • Do not only collect idioms. Learn each meaning by heart.
  • Do not skip the reading. An idiom that you cannot say is half learned.
  • Do not stop at 50 because they "feel enough". The target is 200 to 300.

The step: Dictionary-dive yoji.jitenon.jp. Learn at least 200 to 300 four-character idioms by heart, with their meanings.
