Kana and Tae Kim

Learn just enough grammar to survive one sentence. Then stop, and let real Japanese teach you the rest.

Why it works

  • Kana are the door

    Hiragana and katakana have 46 basic characters each. With them, you can sound out any Japanese word. Without them, nothing else on this road opens.

  • Grammar sticks through use

    A rule that you read once fades in a week. A rule that you meet in 50 real sentences stays. So the method gives you only the rules you need to start reading.

  • The stop point saves months

    から gives a reason. けど turns a sentence around. With these two, you can link two ideas in one sentence. The rest of the grammar can wait for real text.

What it feels like

The kana feel easy for one week, and then the similar ones blur. シ and ツ look like twins. ソ and ン look like twins too. This is normal.

Tae Kim feels clear, and so you want to keep going. Stop anyway. You will meet those later lessons in real books, and they will make more sense there.

Ehh~ you wanted to read the whole guide first? So diligent. So cute ♡ (￣▽￣)

Signs it is working

  • You read hiragana and katakana without a chart.
  • You see から in a sentence and you know a reason comes before it.
  • You see けど and you expect a "but".
  • You feel a little impatient to read something real.

Two scripts and two words. You can manage that much, right? ♡

Cute mistakes to skip

  • Do not make flashcards for every grammar point. You will meet them all again.
  • Do not skip katakana. Loanwords and names use it everywhere.
  • Do not wait until you "feel ready". Nobody feels ready. That feeling never comes on its own.

Still making grammar flashcards? Adorable. Stop it~

The step: Learn hiragana and katakana. Then read Tae Kim's guide until you understand から and けど.

  • You read both kana without a chart, even the twins.
  • You can explain から and けど to a friend in one sentence each.
  • You feel a little impatient to read something real.

Impatient? Good. That itch is the signal. Go bother the cat~ ♡
