FAQ ♡

The questions that every beginner asks. Read them once. Then stop looking for permission and go read the cat.

Before you start

How long does each stage take?

Nobody knows, and the method does not care. A stage ends when its move-on cue is true for you, not when a calendar says so. Stage 1 is usually weeks. Stage 2 is usually months. Stage 5 runs beside stages 2 to 7, so it ends last.

You wanted a number so you could feel behind. No number. No feeling behind~

Do I need a textbook?

No. Tae Kim gives you the grammar you need to start. After that, real texts teach you grammar in context, and Imabi answers the questions that come up. A textbook gives you sentences that nobody wrote for a reason. The cat gives you sentences that a real writer wrote.

Do I need Anki or flashcards?

They are optional. The road repeats words for you through reading. Use cards only where the texts do not repeat enough: four-character idioms in stage 4, 漢文 characters in stage 5, and tones in stage 8. If you use cards, keep each card to one word.

If your deck is bigger than your pages read, you are collecting, not learning~

What about listening and speaking?

The road is reading first, on purpose. Reading gives you the most words per hour, and words are the hard part. Speaking starts in stage 3, and from then on you keep it up beside the reading. Listening comes along for free once you talk to people in VRChat or on Discord.

Is this method for everyone?

No. It is one personal method, and the author learned Japanese from zero with it. It suits people who like hard things and who follow their own curiosity. If you need a teacher to tell you what to do each week, this road will feel lonely.

On the road

Stage 2 is too hard. Can I read something easier first?

You can, but then it is not this method. The point of the cat is that it resets your scale. After it, every book feels lighter. If you read an easy book first, the cat still waits for you at the same height.

Crawl if you must. One sentence a day is still crawling. Quitting is not~ ♡

How do I look up words fast enough?

Use a popup dictionary. Yomitan in the browser, chibipop for games and apps. Hover, read, keep going. Do not write the word down. If it matters, you will meet it again. See the Tools page.

Should I translate each sentence into English?

No. Understand it, or understand most of it, and move on. Translation trains translation. Reading trains reading. You want the second one.

Stage 5 runs beside stages 2 to 7. What does that mean in practice?

Open 般若心経 or 魏志倭人伝 once a week or so, for a few minutes. Read a line, think about it, close it. Your interpretation changes as your Japanese grows. That change is the point.

Why learn four-character idioms at all? People do not talk like that.

They do, more than you think. And the idioms are classical Chinese in miniature. Each one teaches four kanji, their readings, and a piece of the culture. They are the bridge from modern Japanese to stage 5, stage 7, and stage 8. The stage 4 page explains this in full.

Why old texts and 漢文? I want modern Japanese.

Because modern Japanese stands on them. Old grammar hides in set phrases, in formal writing, and in jokes. 漢文 explains why kanji words are built the way they are. And after a week of old text, a modern newspaper feels like a picture book. That feeling is the reward.

I am slow. Am I doing it wrong?

Slow is the method. Fast comes later, on its own, and you do not notice when it arrives. Doing it wrong looks different: swapping texts every week, collecting tools, and reading about Japanese instead of reading Japanese.

Three lines today? Three lines count. Zero lines do not~ (｡•̀ᴗ-)✧

Why does stage 8 start a whole new language?

Because you earned it. The kanji and the classical Chinese that you collected carry straight over. Chinese reading feels strangely easy at first, and only the sounds start from zero. It is also the author's way to say: the road never really ends.

Out of questions? Good. Back to the road~ (≧∀≦)
